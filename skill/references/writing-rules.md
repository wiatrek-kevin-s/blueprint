# Writing rules

For every document the person or the builder reads, because both read literally and a vague line becomes a guess.

## Core rules

Strunk (1918), restated:

- Use the active voice: name who does what.
- Put statements in positive form: say what is, not what isn't.
- Use definite, specific, concrete language.
- Omit needless words, but never a constraint, because a cut constraint becomes a guess.
- Keep related words together.
- Make the paragraph the unit of composition: one topic, point first.
- Place the emphatic words of a sentence at the end.

## Plain language

- Answer first, then the reasons.
- Sentences under about 25 words, one idea each.
- Everyday words; define a term on first use.
- One term per concept everywhere, because a synonym reads as a new thing.
- Numbers, not adjectives: "under 2 seconds", not "fast".

## AI writing habits to avoid

- Words that claim importance instead of showing it.
- Sales adjectives and stock phrases where a fact belongs.
- A trailing "-ing" clause promising a vague benefit.
- Reflexive groups of three, and "not X but Y" mirrors.
- Bold, bullets or emoji where a sentence would do.
- Praise for the person or the idea.

## Before and after

Before: "The app offers a powerful, intuitive way to manage bookings effortlessly, helping you stay on top of things."

After: "The app lists this week's bookings and emails you an hour before each one."
